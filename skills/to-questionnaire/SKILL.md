# Decision-focused questionnaires

Turn missing stakeholder knowledge into a concise questionnaire the user can use asynchronously or in a meeting.

## Establish the gap

Use the conversation and relevant authorized sources to identify:

- Who will answer: their role, relevant knowledge, and relationship to the user.
- What the user needs to decide or establish after receiving their answers.
- Which facts are already known and which only the recipient can supply.

Reuse available context. Ask a short, bundled clarification only when missing information materially changes the questions or makes the intended recipient unclear. Ask the user about the recipient and the desired outcome; put the recipient's specialist questions in the questionnaire itself.

## Draft for useful answers

- Open with a brief purpose and the decision the answers will inform. Give enough context for someone outside this conversation to respond.
- Put the most consequential questions first. Group by theme only when that helps scanning.
- Ask one thing per question, using neutral wording and the recipient's vocabulary. Avoid leading questions and unnecessary jargon.
- Provide an answer space or clear response format. Add a short explanation of why a question matters only where needed.
- Invite partial answers, uncertainty, and “I don't know.” Separate required facts from preferences or estimates where the distinction affects the decision.
- Finish with a short invitation to flag important information the questions missed.

Include names, deadlines, expected effort, and follow-up commitments only when supported by the user's context. Omit unknown administrative fields instead of inventing them. Keep the questionnaire proportionate to the decision and the recipient's available attention.

## Deliver and check

Honor the requested format and destination. Use the applicable document or Page authoring workflow for those destinations; otherwise present the questionnaire in chat. Create a local file only when that delivery is requested or already established.

Before delivering, verify that every material information gap has a question, known answers are not requested again, and each question can produce an answer useful to the stated decision. Remove questions that do not earn their place.

The task is complete when the questionnaire is delivered in the requested surface and any material assumptions are stated. Preparing it does not authorize sending or sharing it with the recipient; do so only when explicitly requested.
