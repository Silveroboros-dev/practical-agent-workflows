description: Evolve an existing product or system through small useful changes, feedback from real use, and revision of earlier design choices. Use when deciding the next increment after a pilot, responding to changed user needs, or when local additions have made the whole system harder to use or change. Skip straightforward scoped edits and feature execution whose direction is already understood.

# Piecemeal Growth

Treat the current design and next-step plan as revisable in light of experience.
Work within the user's objective, constraints, and authority.

- Inspect the current system in its actual context: user outcomes, observed
  friction, operating behavior, and relevant costs of change. Separate direct
  observations from guesses. Use supplied evidence before seeking more.
- Choose the next small change that improves the user's situation or resolves a
  consequential unknown. Keep a tentative direction, but do not expand into a
  master plan for hypothetical needs. A useful increment can remove a feature,
  revise behavior, or repair structure as well as add functionality.
- Keep the system usable and verifiable between increments. Use established
  patterns when they address concrete pressure. Pay attention to dependencies,
  migration costs, and commitments that will be difficult to reverse.
- Obtain feedback appropriate to the decision: actual use when product value is
  uncertain, technical execution when correctness or feasibility is uncertain.
  Tests passing and user satisfaction establish different things. If use has not
  occurred, say so; do not simulate feedback and call it validation.
- Reconsider the whole after a meaningful increment. Keep, revise, remove, or
  replace the provisional choice based on what was learned. A cheap local patch
  is insufficient when it transfers substantial burden elsewhere or entrenches
  a design the evidence has invalidated. Propose larger redesign when repeated
  local repairs cannot address the observed problem, with a bounded migration.
- Continue the authorized task without approval after every slice. Ask only for
  unresolved consequential intent or new authority. If the next decision depends
  on unavailable user feedback, leave that decision pending and complete useful
  independent work. Do not expand scope or change an explicit user commitment
  merely because a different direction seems preferable.

Report briefly: observation, next change and rationale, what the increment will
teach or improve, and the feedback that could change the following step. Reuse
existing task notes. Do not require new ceremonies, services, telemetry systems,
commits, or reports to practice this loop.
